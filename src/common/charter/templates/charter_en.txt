================================================================================
                        DUONET COMMUNITY CHARTER
================================================================================

⚠️ IMPORTANT: This Charter is a SOCIAL CONTRACT, not a legal document.
   It describes the values and expectations of the DuoNet community.
   There is no central authority to enforce it — only the collective choice
   of participants to trust and cooperate with each other.

================================================================================

PREAMBLE

We, the participants of the DuoNet network — developers, server operators,
proxy node owners, and messenger users — taking into account that:

• Technology is a tool, not a subject of legal relations.
• No one shall be deprived of the right to privacy, secrecy of correspondence,
  and free dissemination of information.
• Technology cannot and should not act as a judge in disputes between people.
• Responsibility for the use of the tool lies with the user.
• Development of the technology is possible only under the condition of its
  openness and inalienability.
• Decentralization is the foundation of the service's sustainability and
  accessibility.

Hereby adopt this Charter as a statement of the principles underlying the
DuoNet technology.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 1. STATUS OF TECHNOLOGY — PUBLIC DOMAIN

1.1. The DuoNet technology, including its source code, algorithms, protocols,
and architectural solutions, is recognized as inalienable heritage belonging
to all humanity (public domain).

1.2. No one has the right to: appropriate the technology or its parts; restrict
access to it; hinder its use; register patent or other exclusive rights on its
components.

1.3. Everyone has the right to: study, modify, and distribute the technology;
create new services and applications based on it; use it for any lawful purposes.

1.4. Legally, the status of inalienable heritage is realized through:
distribution of the source code under the GNU Affero General Public License
v3.0 (AGPL-3.0) or any later version; the requirement that any modification
or derivative work retain the status of inalienable heritage; the prohibition
of patenting algorithms and methods implemented in DuoNet.

1.5. Reproducibility. If the original development team ceases the project's
development for any reason, this Charter and the open code serve as the basis
for continuation of development by any interested persons.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 2. PRINCIPLES

2.1. Equality of Participants. All network participants possess equal rights.
Differences in technical roles do not create a hierarchy of rights or privileges.

2.2. Free Basic Functions. Messaging and information protection are always
available to everyone free of charge.

2.3. Voluntary Participation. Participation in the network is voluntary.

2.4. Decentralization. The network has no single center of control.

2.5. User Responsibility. Each participant is independently responsible for
their actions.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 3. RIGHTS OF PARTICIPANTS

3.1. Right to privacy and secrecy of correspondence.
3.2. Right to freely transmit and receive information.
3.3. Right to use own property to provide services to other participants.
3.4. Right to create modifications and distribute them.
3.5. Right to demand compensation for damages.
3.6. Right of server operator to establish additional restrictions on the use
     of their server.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 4. OBLIGATIONS OF PARTICIPANTS

4.1. Respect the rights of other participants.
4.2. Do not use the technology to commit unlawful actions.
4.3. Do not hinder access of others to the technology.
4.4. Maintain the open status of the technology when creating modifications.
4.5. Limit on Server Users. The server operator undertakes not to serve more
     than 450 unique users per day.
4.6. Upon reaching the limit, the server shall direct new users to other
     available servers.
4.7. The proxy server operator is not responsible for the content of traffic
     transmitted through their server.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 5. ACCEPTANCE OF THE CHARTER

5.1. Use of the DuoNet network is possible only after acceptance of this Charter.

5.2. Acceptance is carried out by selecting one of two options in the client
application interface: "I Accept the Charter" or "I Do Not Accept the Charter".

5.3. The fact of acceptance is fixed by a cryptographic signature using the
participant's private key.

5.4. Acceptance of the Charter is voluntary. Refusal entails no consequences
other than the inability to use the network.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 6. LIABILITY AND PRECEDENT

6.1. This Charter does not create a system of moderation, bans, or other
control mechanisms.

6.2. The DuoNet Community is not a legal entity.

6.3. In the event that a participant's actions are recognized as unlawful,
victims have the right to demand compensation, and server operators have the
right to refuse service.

6.4. If DuoNet technology is recognized as illegal in any territory, the
responsible participants bear liability for damages.

6.5. Project Reproduction. Any participant has the right to continue the
project if development ceases.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 7. RELATION TO NATIONAL LEGISLATION

7.1. The participant independently bears responsibility for compliance with
local laws.

7.2. No one has the right to demand that other participants violate their
countries' laws.

================================================================================

ARTICLE 8. AMENDMENTS TO THE CHARTER

8.1. Amendments take effect upon publication. A participant who disagrees may
cease using the network.

8.2. The basic principles in Articles 1–4 cannot be changed.

8.3. Amendments are published in open access.

================================================================================

FINAL PROVISIONS

9.1. This Charter comes into force from the moment of its publication.

9.2. All previous versions become void but are retained in the archive.
